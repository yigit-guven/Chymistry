<h2>About Chymistry</h2>
<p>
  <strong>Chymistry</strong> brings physical, hands-on chemical processing and alchemy to Minecraft. Rather than relying on instantaneous crafting table grids or magical one-block machines, the mod focuses on tangible, in-world manipulation of matter: crushing raw ores and botanical reagents by hand, managing thermodynamics over open heat sources, separating volatile vapors through distillation glassware, and treating materials in aqueous chemical baths.
</p>
<p>
  Every system is designed to reward curiosity, careful observation, and methodical laboratory setup. Whether you are seeking higher-durability alloys, synthesizing corrosive mineral acids, engineering fireproof structural materials, or formulating specialized restorative elixirs, Chymistry integrates into your survival routine as a logical and deeply engaging extension of Minecraft's natural resources.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>The Alchemical Process</h2>
<p>
  Progress in Chymistry is structured around natural physical operations. As you gather new materials and construct specialized apparatuses, each phase of processing opens up new possibilities across your world.
</p>

<div align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yigit-guven/Chymistry/26.2-neoforge/docs/images/blocks/mortarmodel.png" width="96" alt="Mortar and Pestle">
</div>

<h3>1. Mechanical Comminution &amp; Reagents</h3>
<p>
  All chemical synthesis begins with preparation. Raw geological ores, plant cuttings, bone matter, and charcoal cannot easily react in their bulk forms; they must first be reduced to fine, uniform particles. Using a mortar and pestle basin, players grind raw materials down into fine powders, salts, and active fluxes. Establishing a steady physical grinding cadence ensures clean separation and yields the fundamental reagents required for all subsequent metallurgical and alchemical procedures.
</p>

<div align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yigit-guven/Chymistry/26.2-neoforge/docs/images/blocks/irontongs_carry_brickcrucible.png" width="128" alt="Carrying a Crucible with Iron Tongs">
</div>

<h3>2. Pyrometallurgy &amp; Heat Management</h3>
<p>
  Smelting beyond standard furnace capabilities requires controlling thermal environments. Tiered crucibles&mdash;crafted from earthenware brick, heat-hardened deepslate, or resilient netherite&mdash;absorb heat from their surroundings, whether placed atop campfires, blazing fires, molten lava, or specialized sub-zero cooling blocks.
</p>
<p>
  Different chemical reactions and metallurgical blends demand precise operating temperatures. Inattentive operators risk stalling a reaction if temperatures drop too low, or triggering volatile boiling, toxic vapor releases, and catastrophic crucible damage if heat escalates beyond safe thresholds. Dedicated workshop implements like thermometers allow players to monitor thermal levels, while insulated iron tongs enable safely lifting and transporting glowing-hot crucibles around the laboratory without incurring burns.
</p>

<div align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yigit-guven/Chymistry/26.2-neoforge/src/main/resources/assets/chymistry/textures/item/alcohol_bottle.png" width="64" alt="Distillation Product">
</div>

<h3>3. Fractional Distillation &amp; Condensation</h3>
<p>
  Certain compounds cannot survive direct flame and must be isolated based on their boiling points. The alembic functions as a two-block thermal distillation apparatus. When heated from beneath, liquid solutions boil within the retort, driving volatile vapors up through the condenser arm to liquefy and drip into adjacent collection bottles.
</p>
<p>
  This technique unlocks the ability to distill concentrated alcohols, extract organic essences from flora, and concentrate fuming mineral acids such as hydrochloric, nitric, and sulfuric acids. These corrosive distillates serve as potent solvents for precious metal refining, reactive catalysts, and bases for advanced alchemical compounds.
</p>

<div align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yigit-guven/Chymistry/26.2-neoforge/src/main/resources/assets/chymistry/textures/item/rust_powder.png" width="64" alt="Aqueous Chemistry &amp; Reagents">
</div>

<h3>4. Aqueous Chemistry, Leaching &amp; Surface Treatment</h3>
<p>
  Water cauldrons serve as versatile immersion baths for ambient liquid chemistry. Leaving reactive metals submerged in aqueous baths initiates gradual oxidation, yielding fine mineral powders like rust. Beyond corrosion, chemical baths facilitate surface transformations on manufactured goods: stripping dyes and bleaching fabrics back to pure white with synthesized soaps, or soaking wood products in coal-derived creosote to yield permanently fireproof structural timber.
</p>

<div align="center">
  <img src="https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yigit-guven/Chymistry/26.2-neoforge/docs/images/blocks/compostmodel.png" width="80" alt="Production Composting">
</div>

<h3>5. Organic Cultivation &amp; Environmental Applications</h3>
<p>
  The laboratory does not stop at metallurgy and glassware. Repurposing agricultural composters with specialized mineral reagents enables production-scale microbial decomposition, cultivating high-yield fertilizers and concentrated nitrates.
</p>
<p>
  The refined byproducts of chemistry extend directly into survival engineering: crafting blast-proof construction blocks that can absorb explosive shockwaves and neutralize primed explosives, preparing waterproof phosphorus illumination that burns brightly deep underwater, vaporizing aromatic repellent bases to secure perimeters against hostile entities, and brewing potent elixirs that grant full immunity or broad-spectrum restoration in the field.
</p>

<h2>Recipe Lookup &amp; In-Game Guidance</h2>
<p>
  Chymistry features full integration with <strong>Just Enough Items (JEI)</strong>. All recipe types&mdash;including mortar grinding outputs, crucible operating heat windows, distillation condensation outputs, cauldron baths, and wood treatments&mdash;can be looked up directly in-game with dedicated custom interfaces.
</p>
